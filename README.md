# Matthew Levinson

AI capabilities have scaled. Near cutting-edge open-weight models lower the barrier for state and non-state actors to build advanced AI systems for adversarial purposes. The risks from proprietary models with significant safety investments have also increased. Detecting and countering those systems requires production-grade AI safety infrastructure, not just research. For the AI safety community, producing research results, demos, benchmarks, and red teams is no longer enough. Independent and governmental safety organizations need experienced technical leadership that can stay nimble and strategic as teams grow and need to ship at scale. That gap is what motivated my transition into the space.

Before my transition into AI safety research, I spent 6 years leading teams delivering critical consumer-facing ML capabilities at scale, part of a two-decade technical career spanning a PhD in statistics, academic research in economics and computational biology, and nearly 10 years in industry. Over the past 1.5 years, I received fellowships from Coefficient Giving and Simplex AI Safety Research to fund study and new research in technical AI safety. I'm actively looking for the right leadership role.

***Recent Work***<br>

My fellowship research let me build technical fluency at the research frontier at the foundation, the mechanistic underpinnings of the systems the safety community will need to both counter and employ. I explored [increasing atomicity](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03436) of BatchTopK SAEs, and [finding belief geometries](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02685) outside of tightly defined numeric use cases in the representation space of transformers trained on natural language. Code for both are in repos on this account. The [MetaSAE](https://github.com/good-epic/MetaSAE) repo is meant for public use. The repo for [finding belief geometries](https://github.com/good-epic/finding-belief-geometries) is research code.

Earlier in my transition, I did exploratory work on [information storage versus annotated latent computation](https://openreview.net/pdf?id=svMpLjlExR) in a thinking model's chain of thought, [describing activation distributions](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/rzpKYr7xYwHgsccLA/beyond-gaussian-language-model-representations-and) in decoder-only transformers, and investigating [classification through activation distributions](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/6PCjTM55jdYBgHNyp/activation-magnitudes-matter-on-their-own-insights-from-1).
